## Candidate overlap

The following territory is already occupied and must not be presented as the new contribution:

- eigenvalue/Hessian-based degeneracy detection and solution projection;
- fine-grained/direction-wise localizability classification;
- predictive observability/confidence from raw correspondence geometry;
- adaptive LIO parameters for narrow/corridor environments;
- broad benchmarking of SLAM in tunnels, corridors, stairs, and other degenerate scenes;
- the theoretical distinction between current local observability and previously accumulated global drift;
- repeated-run reproducibility as a concern in LIO.
- directional degeneracy masks over time, over/under-detection, detection-ratio summaries, and registration recall at fixed per-pair error cutoffs;
- RTE/ATE time series over annotated degenerate spans and field/simulation demonstrations that recovery is feasible when informative geometry returns.

## Defensible candidate distinction, pending R1

The narrower candidate is an **event-level test of the relationship between an online health value and sustained local trajectory accuracy after geometry exit**. Existing work above directly overlaps with the scene transition, time-varying detector output, registration recall, and RTE/ATE pieces. What was not found in the inspected articles/supplement is their combination into a causal, sustained post-exit decision evaluation that:

1. geometry exit is annotated independently of the indicator and estimator error;
2. short-window translation and rotation recovery is defined from independent reference as a sustained hindsight outcome;
3. the online indicator is evaluated for false reassurance, missed recovery, availability, and recovery-detection delay;
4. original-frame accumulated drift remains a separate reported outcome; and
5. operating rules are frozen on development trajectories and transferred to unseen scenes.

This is a **candidate empirical distinction**, not a novelty or “first” claim. The ICA supplementary-video contents were not accessible; the full paper and official implementation README were reviewed. DCReg's full supplement was reviewed. The candidate still needs a fair comparator, frozen outcomes, multiple scene clusters and independent R2/R3 review before any publication claim.
